Model rules, constraints and limitations

How to use FlowSim

  1. Enter your starting position: investments, existing debt and cash. An optional upfront contribution moves money from your starting cash into investments; it does not add new wealth.
  2. Enter your monthly plan: take-home pay, recurring bills, cash to set aside and the percentage of remaining pay to invest. Review the return, borrowing-rate and growth assumptions. The sample values are examples, not recommendations.
  3. Choose your limits and optional scenarios: set the debt limits and interest-coverage minimum described below. You can delay or disable bill borrowing, change contributions later, or add a stress scenario.
  4. Explore the results: the plan updates automatically as you edit; there is no Run button. Read the summary and warnings first. Hover or tap the chart, or use the month slider and arrow keys, to inspect individual months. Use the ledger for the full breakdown and CSV or Print / PDF to export it.

How each month works

  1. The model applies any scheduled investment drop, then price growth, and calculates cash distributions from those investments.
  2. It borrows for recurring bills up to the limits you selected. This month's new pay contributions do not increase that month's borrowing capacity.
  3. Bills not covered by borrowing use take-home pay first, then existing cash if you allow it. If bills still cannot be paid, the projection stops before that month. Displayed balances include completed months only.
  4. From the pay left after bills, the model sets aside your requested cash amount if enough is available, then invests your chosen percentage of the remainder. Any pay not invested stays in cash, which earns no return here.
  5. Investment distributions pay loan interest first. Any remaining distributions reduce debt toward your target, then are reinvested. Unpaid interest is added to the debt.

A simple pay example: suppose take-home pay is $5,000, bills are $3,500, and the model permits $1,000 of bill borrowing. The other $2,500 of bills comes from pay, leaving $2,500. If you set aside $500 in cash and invest 100% of the rest, $2,000 is contributed and $500 stays in cash. This illustrates pay routing only; distributions and loan interest are handled separately.

What the limits mean

  • DTA means debt divided by investments. Cash is not included in this ratio. For example, $20,000 of debt against $100,000 of investments is 20% DTA.
  • DTA paydown target: guides how remaining distributions are split between debt repayment and reinvestment. The model does not borrow just to reach this target, and the target is not a guaranteed debt level.
  • DTA draw guardrail: limits new bill borrowing using investments before this month's pay contributions and before debt repayment, including any unpaid interest added to debt. It cannot guarantee that an existing balance stays below the limit.
  • Cash-interest coverage floor: compares investment distributions with loan interest, not with household bills. A 3× floor means $3 of distributions for each $1 of interest. It is a limit you choose, not lender approval.

What the projection cannot tell you

  • Passing the model's checks does not establish a safe long-term borrowing budget or prove you can retire. Compare different assumptions and review warnings before relying on a result.
  • Stage dates show the first time a condition is reached. Stages can be skipped or reversed. The optional reduced-contribution stage changes how much pay you invest; it does not automatically stop your pay or prove financial independence.
  • The model follows your selected assumptions, not a forecast of unpredictable markets. A chosen stress scenario is only one possible path. Taxes, fees, lender eligibility, actual credit limits, maintenance requirements and forced liquidation are not modeled.
  • Borrowing pays bills but creates debt; it is not income or investment profit. This app has no account connections and executes no transfers, trades or loans.
Advanced details: timing, stress and warning rules

Timing: annual rates are divided by 12 for monthly calculations. Monthly compounding means the full-year change can differ from the entered annual rate. Pay growth and bill inflation begin in month 2; price growth begins in month 1 and excludes distributions. Distributions are calculated before new pay contributions and used only once. A full month of interest is charged on opening debt plus that month's bill borrowing. Adding unpaid interest to debt is a hypothetical accounting assumption, not a promise about lender treatment.

Stress: the investment price drop happens once, before growth in the chosen month. The yield cut and APR increase continue afterward; the pay cut lasts only the entered duration. Yield and pay cuts are proportional reductions, while the APR increase is in percentage points. Random market volatility and repeated shocks are not simulated.

Warnings and undefined ratios: zero interest makes coverage N/A. Zero investments makes DTA N/A, not a safe ratio. Existing debt can breach limits even with no new borrowing; contributions and distribution-funded repayments may help repair it, but recovery is not guaranteed. An unmet cash allocation produces a warning rather than stopping the projection. Unpaid bills stop it; no extra borrowing, asset sale or income is invented.

Scope: these are the model's rules, not a lender's rules. No FYNANC stage thresholds are assumed, and M1 smart transfers are not automatically recurring margin borrowing.
