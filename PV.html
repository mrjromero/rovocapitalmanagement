Understanding Your Metrics

Positive Carry Portfolio: An investment strategy where the income yield generated by your assets consistently exceeds the cost of borrowing (margin interest) used to acquire them, creating a self-sustaining cash flow engine.

Multiplier: Represents your total assets per $1 of your own equity. A 1.33x multiplier means you control $1.33 in the market for every $1 you own.

DSCR: Measures if your portfolio's income covers its margin interest. A ratio above 1.0x means your debt is paying for itself without you injecting cash.

DTA (Debt-to-Asset): The strict percentage of your total portfolio funded by borrowed money. Lower means less risk.

Margin Utilization: How much of your borrowing limit you are actively using. Withdrawal assumes taking cash out of the account, while Reinvestment assumes buying more stock (which adds its own equity buffer to your limit).

Margin Health: The exact portfolio drop required to wipe out your excess equity, resulting in a margin call. The dollar figure shows the absolute market value loss at that threshold.

M1 Margin Safety Guide

The standard maintenance requirement at M1 Finance is 30% of account value. However, M1 regularly imposes higher limits (up to 100%) for riskier positions, concentrated holdings, or high-volatility derivative income funds. If requirements alter dynamically, assets can be liquidated automatically without notice.

1. M1 Portfolio Drop Formula

Max Drop % = 1 - [ (Margin Debt - Cash) / (Portfolio Value × (1 - R)) ]

Where R equals your blended regulatory maintenance requirement percentage.

The 50% Rule: A standard risk mitigation target is leaving at least half of your available borrowing power completely un-utilized. For typical high-yield setups, this allows the total engine to withstand a 35% to 45% sudden broad market drawdown before breaching safety floors.

Volatility Matching: If you tilt heavily toward specialized derivative-income plays or individual equities, simulate your cushions assuming a severe structural baseline requirement requirement of 40% or 50% instead of the standard 30%.

Historical Drawdown Buffer: Trace the maximum technical peak-to-trough drops of your core funds historically (e.g., ~20% drops for indices, 30%+ for sector options). Structure your loan balance so your threshold sits 5 to 10 points lower than structural worst-case history.

Establishing strict behavioral ceilings on borrowing volume remains the most reliable buffer. For example, a rigorously modeled engine might target a deep 74.02% portfolio drop threshold, ensuring the system remains self-sustaining through extreme macro events without forced deposits.
