CarryPilot Quantitative Methodology Guide

Operational Risk Thresholds, Volatility Term Structures, and Adaptive Leverage Mechanics

1. Executive Summary

CarryPilot operates an adaptive, quantitative risk framework designed for leveraged dividend, credit carry, and derivative overlay portfolios. Rather than enforcing rigid, hardcoded Loan-to-Value (LTV) constraints, the engine measures systemic fragility in real time across four institutional pillars:

  • Volatility Term Structure: Spot VIX divided by 3-Month VIX (^VIX / ^VIX3M).
  • Broad Market Momentum: S&P 500 Price vs. its native 50-Day Moving Average (SPX vs 50d SMA).
  • Multi-Tier Credit Solvency: High-Yield to Investment-Grade ratio (HYG / LQD) and Senior Loan performance (BKLN).
  • Short-Term Funding Plumbing: 10-Year Treasury Yield vs. 13-Week T-Bill (^TNX - ^IRX) in basis points.

3. Quantitative Trigger Breakdown

🟢 Green (Smooth Waters) • Safety Score: 8–10

Occurs when the volatility term structure is in Contango (VIX / VIX3M < 0.90) and equities trade comfortably above their 50-day moving average. Low forward implied volatility allows derivative overlay engines (SPYI, QDTE, FEPI) and senior floating credit (JAAA, CLOI) to generate clean positive spread arbitrage against borrow rates without capital erosion risk.

🟡 Yellow (Watchful / Caution) • Safety Score: 5–7

Triggered when any of the following conditions emerge:

  • The volatility curve flattens into the caution band (0.90 ≤ Ratio ≤ 1.00).
  • The S&P 500 breaks below its 50-day SMA, indicating distribution and negative dealer gamma risks.
  • The 5-day rolling trend engine detects a rapid deterioration (VIX ratio delta > +0.05 vs 5d avg).
  • High-yield spreads diverge from equities, signaling corporate refinancing frictions.
🔴 Red / 🟠 Orange (Storm / De-risking) • Safety Score: 1–4

Triggered when the volatility curve undergoes Backwardation / Inversion (VIX / VIX3M > 1.00). Near-term options cost more than 3-month options, signaling institutional panic. Combined with a sub-50d SMA equity posture and widening credit spreads, this mandate requires immediate margin debt reduction to defend against collateral shrinkage.

4. Balance Sheet Architecture

The CarryPilot model divides assets into two complementary engines:

  • Collateral Floor (Ballast): Ultra-short Treasuries (SGOV, TBIL, CSHI) and AAA senior floating CLOs (JAAA, CLOI) that trade with minimal volatility, clearing the borrow hurdle rate and shielding capital from drawdowns.
  • Income Engine (Propulsion): Diversified covered call ETFs and Business Development Companies (SPYI, QDTE, ARCC) that harvest elevated cash-flow yields to compound net equity.
