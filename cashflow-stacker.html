The "When to Increase Stack Size" settings act as the throttle for the Cash Flow Stacking strategy. They dictate exactly when and how aggressively you scale up your investments as your cash flow grows. Whenever one of the three triggers below is hit, the system takes the base cost of your previous stack and multiplies it by your Stack Increase Multiplier.

1. Payoff Threshold (Performance-Based Scaling)

  • The Logic: "Increase the stack size if the LOC is paid off within a certain number of months."
  • How it works: This is a dynamic, velocity-based trigger. The system tracks a stopwatch every time you buy a stack. It counts exactly how many months it takes for your combined income (personal + stack income) to drive the Line of Credit (LOC) debt back to zero. If you clear the debt in less than or equal to your defined threshold, the system determines your cash flow is strong enough to handle a larger asset, and applies the multiplier to the next purchase.
  • Best For: Optimizing for maximum safe growth. It automatically speeds up your scaling as your compounding income accelerates, but acts as a safety brake if your payoff times slow down.

2. Every Certain Number of Stacks Created (Volume-Based Scaling)

  • The Logic: "Increase the stack size every X stacks created."
  • How it works: This creates a step-ladder approach based purely on the number of investment cycles you complete, regardless of how long they take. If you set the threshold to 4, your 1st, 2nd, and 3rd stacks will all be deployed at the initial base size. The moment you purchase your 4th stack, the multiplier kicks in.
  • Best For: Investors who want a predictable, phased approach. It forces you to complete a specific number of "reps" at a lower risk level before graduating to larger asset purchases.

3. Every Certain Number of Months (Time-Based Scaling)

  • The Logic: "Create a new larger stack every X months."
  • How it works: This logic is tied strictly to the simulation's calendar. It completely ignores how many stacks you've bought or how fast you are paying them off. If you set the threshold to 12, the base size of any stack you purchase will automatically increase on the anniversary of the simulation.
  • Best For: Mimicking traditional annual portfolio growth, or matching stack sizes to anticipated future life events.

The "Use Credit Balance for Larger Stacks" Mechanic

This checkbox is an advanced capital efficiency tool designed to ensure you never have "lazy money" sitting idle. Because cash flow compounds rapidly in this strategy, there are often months where your income completely pays off the LOC debt and leaves you with a positive cash surplus before you actually deploy the next stack.

  • If Unchecked (Strict Sizing): The system will only purchase a stack at the exact base size calculated by the multiplier. If the formula says your next stack should be $20,000, and you happen to have a $5,000 positive cash balance in the account, the system borrows the $20,000, leaving your $5,000 cash sitting uninvested.
  • If Checked (Sweep & Deploy): The system sweeps any positive cash balance and adds it on top of the calculated stack size. Using the same scenario above, the system takes the $20,000 calculated size, adds your $5,000 cash surplus, and deploys a custom $25,000 stack.

By checking this box, you effectively purchase a larger, higher-yielding asset without actually taking on any deeper LOC debt, maximizing the mathematical compounding effect of the strategy.
