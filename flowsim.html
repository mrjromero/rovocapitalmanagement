Returns and financing
Scenario guardrails

DSCR = asset cash flow divided by required debt service. This FLOW margin scenario assumes no scheduled principal, so coverage is portfolio distributions divided by interest. Voluntary paydowns are not required debt service; other household debts are excluded. Capitalized interest still counts as a cost and increases debt. Zero interest is N/A, not an automatic pass.

DTA (Debt-to-Asset Ratio) = margin debt divided by investment assets; separate cash is excluded. It is not M1 credit utilization or Margin Health. Fixed mode checks this threshold without changing routing. Adaptive mode uses it to screen new modeled bill financing. It does not rebalance investments and is not a lender maintenance limit or a safe borrowing recommendation. Undefined ratios show N/A.

FLOW, Maximum Contribution and M1 terms

Financing selected bills illustrates Systematic Bill Redirection: less direct bill payment can leave more household cash for contribution. This does not implement the full Maximum Contribution sequence or prove that all income can be contributed.

Freedom Margin Line is the education term; M1 calls the product a Margin Loan. This model does not simulate the Income Hub, Sweep or Bill Pay accounts, contribution-first margin paydown, or broker cash-first interest billing. The interest selector is a scenario policy. M1 uses available account cash first; a shortfall can increase the margin loan subject to capacity, with possible securities sales if funding is insufficient. Its daily billing is not reproduced by this monthly approximation.

APR and distribution yield are assumptions, not Maximum Target Interest (MTI) or Minimum Target Yield (MTY) thresholds. Price growth excludes distributions; total return combines both. Cash distributions are not proof of economic profit.

M1 Available to Borrow, credit utilization and Margin Health describe broker capacity and risk; none is Safety Buffer B2 or the DTA guardrail. M1 dashboard terms · M1 interest billing

What this model does, and does not, say

  • Each month: apply any shock; calculate distributions and price changes on opening investments; calculate interest; pay direct bills; invest available paycheck cash; use remaining cash/distributions to repay debt; reinvest any excess.
  • Returns use annual rates divided by 12. Pay and all bills grow monthly at the entered annual rate divided by 12. This is a deterministic illustration, not a forecast.
  • Interest is charged on opening debt plus this month’s bill borrowing for a full month. New investments do not earn until next month. The exact advantage of early investing and later bill borrowing is not modeled.
  • Cash yield and price growth are separate. Do not enter total return as price growth. A distribution is not proof of economic profit or sustainable income.
  • Taxes, fees, loan principal schedules, lender eligibility, maintenance requirements and daily settlement are excluded. Coverage means distributions divided by interest only, not household debt-service coverage.
  • Funding failures stop a path. Limit breaches are warnings, not forced liquidation. No automatic emergency strategy is assumed. A completed path or a limit pass is not a safety certification.
  • The $250 starting bill-financing sample is for learning, not a recommended borrowing amount. The 7% distribution yield, 2% price growth and 6% APR are round assumptions, not forecasts or live quotes. Reserves must be assessed separately. Nothing here executes trades, transfers, borrowing or payments.

Calculations stay in this browser. No analytics, account linking or automatic storage. CSV downloads may contain figures you enter: keep them private. “Noindex” discourages search indexing; it is not access control.
