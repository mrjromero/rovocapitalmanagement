What this model does, and does not, say

  • Each month: apply any shock; calculate distributions and price changes on opening investments; calculate interest; pay direct bills; invest available paycheck cash; use remaining cash/distributions to repay debt; reinvest any excess.
  • Returns use annual rates divided by 12. Pay and all bills grow monthly at the entered annual rate divided by 12. This is a deterministic illustration, not a forecast.
  • Interest is charged on opening debt plus this month’s bill borrowing for a full month. New investments do not earn until next month. The exact advantage of early investing and later bill borrowing is not modeled.
  • Cash yield and price growth are separate. Do not enter total return as price growth. A distribution is not proof of economic profit or sustainable income.
  • Taxes, fees, loan principal schedules, lender eligibility, maintenance requirements and daily settlement are excluded. Coverage means distributions divided by interest only, not household debt-service coverage.
  • Funding failures stop a path. Limit breaches are warnings, not forced liquidation. No automatic emergency strategy is assumed. A completed path or a limit pass is not a safety certification.
  • The $900 sample fits its steady baseline, not every stress. Reserves must be assessed separately. Nothing here executes trades, transfers, borrowing or payments.

Calculations stay in this browser. No analytics, account linking or automatic storage. CSV downloads may contain figures you enter: keep them private. “Noindex” discourages search indexing; it is not access control.
