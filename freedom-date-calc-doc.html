Technical Reference Manual: Freedom Date Calculator Engine

Applies to: freedom-date-calc.html

This manual describes the upgraded projection engine used to compare Traditional (Linear Savings & Drawdown) and Stacking (Positive-Carry Capital Amplifier). It documents the cash-flow rules, leverage constraints, forced-deleveraging behavior, optimization rules, and first-passage Freedom Date statistics implemented by the application.

1. Core Parameters

  • $I$: Total monthly gross income ($/mo)
  • $E_0$: Baseline monthly living expenses ($/mo)
  • $E_m$: Inflation-adjusted monthly living expenses at month $m$
  • $Q_0$: Starting portfolio net equity ($)
  • $Q_{m,s}$: Net equity at month $m$ for simulation path $s$
  • $A_{m,s}$: Gross portfolio assets at month $m$ for simulation path $s$
  • $D_{m,s}$: Margin debt at month $m$ for simulation path $s$
  • $i_{inf}$: Annual inflation rate
  • $r_a$: Expected annual capital-appreciation rate
  • $y$: Annual cash-distribution yield
  • $r_m$: Annual margin interest rate
  • $L_{DTA}$: Strategic target debt-to-assets ratio
  • $L_{max}$: Brokerage maximum debt-to-assets borrowing limit
  • $M_{maint}$: Minimum equity percentage required by maintenance margin
  • $\sigma_{ann}$: Annualized price volatility

2. Rate Conversion and Market Returns

Annual assumptions are converted to monthly discrete rates:

$$\begin{aligned} r_{inf,m} &= (1+i_{inf})^{1/12}-1 \\ \mu_{app,m} &= \frac{r_a}{12} \\ y_m &= \frac{y}{12} \\ r_{margin,m} &= \frac{r_m}{12} \\ \sigma_m &= \frac{\sigma_{ann}}{\sqrt{12}} \end{aligned}$$

When volatility is enabled, the paired monthly appreciation shock used by both modes for path $s$ is:

$$R_{app,m,s}=\mu_{app,m}+Z_{m,s}\sigma_m,\qquad Z_{m,s}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,1)$$

When volatility is disabled, $\sigma_m=0$ and all 500 paths use the deterministic appreciation rate $\mu_{app,m}$.

3. Input Validation and Cross-Field Constraints

The engine rejects invalid assumptions instead of silently replacing or clamping them. The implemented ranges are:

InputAllowed Range
Income, expenses, starting equity$\ge 0$
Current age0 to 120
Expected annual appreciation-100% to 100%
Annual volatility0% to 300%
Annual inflationGreater than -100%, up to 100%
Cash yieldGreater than 0%, up to 100%
Margin interest rate0% to 100%
Strategic DTA0% to 89%
Maximum borrowing limit0% to 100%
Maintenance margin0% to 99%

The brokerage constraints imply a maximum allowable debt-to-assets ratio:

$$L_{guard}=\min\left(L_{max},\,1-M_{maint}\right)$$

A configuration is valid only when:

$$L_{DTA}\le L_{guard}$$ $$y_{net}=y-(L_{DTA}\cdot r_m)>0$$

The second rule requires positive net portfolio yield after the modeled cost of margin debt.

4. Freedom Targets

The target is the net equity required for portfolio cash flow to cover inflation-adjusted living expenses without relying on employment income.

Traditional Target

$$E_m=E_0(1+r_{inf,m})^m$$ $$Q_{target,trad}(m)=\frac{E_m\cdot12}{y}$$

Stacking Target

At target leverage, net cash flow on one dollar of net equity reflects distributions on gross assets less margin interest:

$$y_{net}=y-(L_{DTA}\cdot r_m)$$ $$Q_{target,stack}(m)=\frac{(E_m\cdot12)(1-L_{DTA})}{y_{net}}$$

Unlike the prior engine, the upgraded version does not replace a non-positive $y_{net}$ with an artificial minimum. It rejects the configuration.

5. Monthly Iteration Engines

Mode 1: Traditional Engine

Traditional mode holds an unleveraged portfolio. Income pays living expenses, and the remaining surplus or deficit changes portfolio equity:

$$\Delta C_m=I-E_m$$ $$Q_{m,s}=\max\left(0,\;Q_{m-1,s} +(Q_{m-1,s}R_{app,m,s}) +(Q_{m-1,s}y_m) +\Delta C_m\right)$$

A positive $\Delta C_m$ is invested. A negative $\Delta C_m$ represents assets used to fund the lifestyle deficit.

Mode 2: Stacking Engine

Stacking separates gross assets, debt, and net equity:

$$A_0=\frac{Q_0}{1-L_{DTA}},\qquad D_0=A_0-Q_0,\qquad Q=A-D$$
Monthly stacking sequence:
1. Apply price appreciation to gross assets.
2. Apply expenses, margin interest, employment income, and portfolio distributions to debt exactly once.
3. Invest any cash remaining after debt reaches zero.
4. Enforce brokerage guardrails and forced deleveraging when required.
5. Reuse borrowing capacity when leverage is below the strategic target.

5.1 Asset Evolution

$$A^*_{m,s}=A_{m-1,s}(1+R_{app,m,s})$$

5.2 Debt Sweep

$$\text{Distribution Income}=A_{m-1,s}y_m$$ $$\text{Margin Interest}=D_{m-1,s}r_{margin,m}$$ $$D^*_{m,s}=D_{m-1,s}+E_m+\text{Margin Interest}-I-\text{Distribution Income}$$

Employment income and distributions reduce debt once. The former floatJuice adjustment has been removed because it credited an additional return on income without adding corresponding invested assets.

5.3 Excess Cash After Debt Repayment

If the debt sweep produces $D^*_{m,s}<0$, the negative balance is treated as excess cash and invested:

$$A^*_{m,s}\leftarrow A^*_{m,s}+|D^*_{m,s}|,\qquad D^*_{m,s}\leftarrow0$$

5.4 Guardrail Enforcement and Forced Deleveraging

Calculate provisional equity and leverage:

$$Q^*_{m,s}=A^*_{m,s}-D^*_{m,s},\qquad L_{curr}=\frac{D^*_{m,s}}{A^*_{m,s}}$$

If $Q^*_{m,s}\le0$, or an extreme return produces invalid gross assets, the account is liquidated to zero for that month.

If $L_{curr}>L_{guard}$, the model records a brokerage guardrail event and sells assets to repay debt. Let:

$$L_{recovery}=\min(L_{DTA},L_{guard})$$ $$S=\frac{D^*_{m,s}-(L_{recovery}A^*_{m,s})}{1-L_{recovery}}$$ $$A_{m,s}=A^*_{m,s}-S,\qquad D_{m,s}=D^*_{m,s}-S$$

If the required sale is invalid or consumes the full asset balance, the account is liquidated. This makes downside brokerage constraints part of each stochastic path rather than display-only metrics.

5.5 Strategic Releveraging

If no guardrail is breached and $L_{curr}<L_{DTA}$, borrowing capacity is reused:

$$A_{target}=\frac{Q^*_{m,s}}{1-L_{DTA}}$$ $$\Delta D_{borrow}=\max(0,A_{target}-A^*_{m,s})$$ $$A_{m,s}=A^*_{m,s}+\Delta D_{borrow}$$ $$D_{m,s}=D^*_{m,s}+\Delta D_{borrow}$$

The borrowing occurs only if the resulting DTA remains within $L_{guard}$. If $L_{DTA}\le L_{curr}\le L_{guard}$, the provisional asset and debt balances are retained.

6. Risk and Liquidity Metrics

Credit-Line Utilization

$$\text{Credit Line}=A\cdot L_{max}$$ $$\text{Utilization}=\frac{D}{A\cdot L_{max}}\cdot100\%$$

Zero debt reports 0% utilization. Positive debt with no available credit line is invalid rather than being reported as 0% utilization. The dashboard highlights utilization above 70%.

Drop to Maintenance-Margin Call

$$A_{call}=\frac{D}{1-M_{maint}}$$ $$\text{Drop to Call}=\frac{A-A_{call}}{A}\cdot100\%$$

An unleveraged account reports a 100% cushion. The dashboard highlights cushions below 25%.

Simulated Guardrail Event Rate

The stacking narrative reports the percentage of simulation paths that required forced deleveraging at least once:

$$\text{Event Rate}=\frac{\#\{s:\text{path }s\text{ had one or more guardrail breaches}\}}{N}\cdot100\%$$

7. Auto-Optimization Engine

optimizeArchitecture() performs a bounded search:

  • $L_{DTA}\in[15\%,35\%]$, step 2.5%
  • $y\in[8\%,15\%]$, step 0.5%

A candidate is considered only when all of these rules pass:

  1. $L_{DTA}\le L_{guard}$
  2. Gross distribution income exceeds margin interest
  3. Credit-line utilization is finite and no more than 70%
  4. Drop-to-call cushion is at least 25%
  5. Estimated monthly net cash flow is positive
$$A_0=\frac{Q_0}{1-L_{DTA}},\qquad D_0=A_0-Q_0$$ $$\text{Net Spread}=A_0y-D_0r_m$$ $$\text{Monthly Net Cash Flow}=I-E_0+\frac{\text{Net Spread}}{12}$$ $$m_{est}=\max\left(0,\frac{Q_{target,stack}(0)-Q_0}{\text{Monthly Net Cash Flow}}\right)$$

The appreciation assumption selected with the winning yield remains:

$$r_a=\begin{cases} 2.5\% & y\ge12\%\\ 3.5\% & 10\%\le y<12\%\\ 4.5\% & y<10\% \end{cases}$$

If no safe candidate exists, the optimizer displays a message and leaves all user inputs unchanged. The winning candidate is then evaluated by the full 30-year simulation; the optimizer's objective is only a screening estimate.

8. Monte Carlo and Freedom Date Statistics

  • Path count: $N=500$
  • Duration: $M=360$ months
  • Pairing: Traditional and stacking receive the same market shock for a given month and path.

Monthly Equity Percentiles for the Chart

At each month, equity across all paths is sorted to produce the 10th, 50th, and 90th percentile balance curves. These are chart bands only; they are not used as Freedom Date percentiles.

First-Passage Freedom Dates

For every path and mode, the engine records the first month in which equity meets that mode's contemporaneous inflation-adjusted target:

$$T_s=\min\{m:Q_{m,s}\ge Q_{target}(m)\}$$

If a path does not meet the target within 360 months, it is right-censored as greater than 30 years. The application then sorts the 500 first-hit dates:

  • Optimistic Date: 10th percentile of $T_s$
  • Expected Freedom Date: 50th percentile of $T_s$
  • Conservative Date: 90th percentile of $T_s$

If the selected percentile falls among censored paths, the dashboard displays >30 Years. This first-passage method ensures the reported dates describe trajectory outcomes rather than crossings of unrelated monthly equity percentiles.
