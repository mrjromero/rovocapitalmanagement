Calculation rules & limitations

Each interval runs from the previous observation to the next calendar month-end, with a final partial interval to the selected target date. The starting stage determines that interval’s annual nominal return. Growth compounds over actual elapsed days using a 365.2425-day year, then the contribution is added. The first full contribution is at the first month-end strictly after the starting date; it is not prorated for a short first month. No contribution is made on a partial final month.

After every 12 contributions, the monthly contribution increases by the selected annual percentage. Nominal equity and each contribution are converted to starting-date dollars using the inflation assumption. Stages are re-evaluated from real equity at each observation and can move down as well as up. A crossing changes the return assumption for the next interval; milestone dates are monthly estimates, not exact daily crossings. Stage 5 continues the accumulation scenario beyond 100%; household support and deleveraging are not yet modeled.

The dashed path solves for one constant real annual return that, with the same nominal contributions and inflation, reaches the real target on the selected date. The solver searches −99% to 1,000% annual real return and reports when no solution exists in that range. Its implied nominal return is (1 + real return) × (1 + inflation) − 1. The modeled path instead uses each stage’s editable nominal return.

Real investment growth is ending real equity minus starting equity minus inflation-adjusted contributions. It includes the purchasing-power effect of inflation. Required growth is not shown when the target is already reached. Projection horizons are limited to 100 years; extreme scenarios beyond the numerical display range are rejected. Historical yield is context only and is not added to total return.

03 / Research context

Evidence & validation

The architecture was stress-tested in the FYNANC STAGES research using modern PortfoliosLab implementations, 2020-era proxy portfolios, 2008-capable proxies, and explicit hedge-vs-no-hedge comparisons. These observations informed the settled stage design; they do not establish statistical certainty or guarantee future protection.

Modern implementations

Tested the working ticker combinations and their recent return, income, diversification, and drawdown trade-offs. Modern funds have limited common history, so these results do not cover every crisis.

Longer-history proxies

2020-era proxies broadened the stress lens; 2008-capable proxies examined a deeper credit and equity crisis. Substitutes test the architecture's behavior, not the exact historical performance of today's ETFs.

Explicit hedge comparisons

Modern 5% DBMF tests supported keeping a modest hedge in Stages 1–3. Crisis proxies supported the protective role of managed futures, but do not prove an identical benefit for an exact 5% allocation in every crisis.

Selected recorded observations & evidence limits

The conversation's Stage 2 comparison reported 1-year volatility falling from 5.10% to 4.74%, all-period maximum drawdown improving from −8.10% to −7.55%, and downside capture falling from 14.84% to 5.14%. Full-period annualized return fell from 10.63% to 10.17%. These are recorded historical test results, not live statistics or inputs to the planner.

The Stage 1 and Stage 3 modern hedge tests used the common period August 30, 2022–October 7, 2026, quarterly rebalancing, and an S&P 500 Total Return benchmark. Their recorded annualized returns were 10.03% and 10.05%; their 1-year Sharpe ratios were 1.41 and 1.69. Return, volatility, capture, and drawdown measurements use different windows and should not be conflated.

This page summarizes the supplied research conclusions and all five modern portfolio exports. It does not reproduce the full return histories, proxy substitutions, or all test assumptions, and does not independently rerun or audit the backtests. Historical comparisons are sensitive to dates, proxy choices, and implementation assumptions. No optimality claim is made.
