Calculation rules & limitations

Start from the actual balances. Assets minus outstanding debt equals net equity. The observed starting DTA is preserved until the first modeled month-end. A 25% target DTA implies assets equal to 1.333× net equity after each successful reset. No current account values are invented or fetched automatically by this standalone page.

Monthly sequence: Apply the current stage’s annual nominal total return to opening assets over the elapsed days. Estimate distributions as opening assets × annual distribution yield × elapsed years, and subtract those distributions from total-return gains to obtain the price-change component. This keeps total return intact. Interest accrues on opening debt at APR × actual days / 365; the optional rate-change date splits the interest calculation when it falls inside an interval.

In the default routing, distributions are reinvested, interest is added to margin debt, $15,000 of contributions buys assets, and the $7,000 household withdrawal adds to margin debt. The alternative routing pays interest and household spending from distributions first, reinvests any remainder, and borrows any shortfall. Turning withdrawals off models accumulation. Monthly contributions and withdrawals occur at the first month-end strictly after the starting date, without prorating the first payment. Contributions may step up after every 12 deposits; withdrawals remain fixed nominal dollars.

Restore DTA: After the month’s flows, calculate equity E = assets − debt. Set target debt to E × DTA / (1 − DTA). If target debt exceeds existing debt, borrow the difference and invest it; otherwise sell assets and repay the difference. Assets and debt move equally, so restoration itself does not change equity. Restoring an exact target can require asset sales even when household withdrawals were initially funded from margin. The model assumes those trades and borrowings are available.

The main path uses 4.40% borrowing until an optional change date, then 5.65%. With no change date, it stays at the current rate; the separate comparison uses the future rate throughout. All rates are editable. A higher-rate comparison is a scenario, not a scheduled change. Spread means annual portfolio total return minus borrowing APR; it is not spendable cash flow. The distributions-minus-interest cash flow is separately visible in the monthly ledger.

Stages follow inflation-adjusted net equity at each observation and can move down. New stage returns apply in the next interval. A partial final month accrues growth and interest but has no monthly contribution, withdrawal, or DTA reset. The selected DTA policy continues through Stage 5; automatic long-term deleveraging is not assumed. Projection stops when equity becomes nonpositive. It does not simulate broker liquidation thresholds. Broker requirements can differ from this planning ratio; see the SEC explanation of margin borrowing.

03 / Research context

Evidence & validation

The architecture was stress-tested in the FYNANC STAGES research using modern PortfoliosLab implementations, 2020-era proxy portfolios, 2008-capable proxies, and explicit hedge-vs-no-hedge comparisons. These observations informed the settled stage design; they do not establish statistical certainty or guarantee future protection.

Modern implementations

Tested the working ticker combinations and their recent return, income, diversification, and drawdown trade-offs. Modern funds have limited common history, so these results do not cover every crisis.

Longer-history proxies

2020-era proxies broadened the stress lens; 2008-capable proxies examined a deeper credit and equity crisis. Substitutes test the architecture's behavior, not the exact historical performance of today's ETFs.

Explicit hedge comparisons

Modern 5% DBMF tests supported keeping a modest hedge in Stages 1–3. Crisis proxies supported the protective role of managed futures, but do not prove an identical benefit for an exact 5% allocation in every crisis.

Selected recorded observations & evidence limits

The conversation's Stage 2 comparison reported 1-year volatility falling from 5.10% to 4.74%, all-period maximum drawdown improving from −8.10% to −7.55%, and downside capture falling from 14.84% to 5.14%. Full-period annualized return fell from 10.63% to 10.17%. These are recorded historical test results, not live statistics or inputs to the planner.

The Stage 1 and Stage 3 modern hedge tests used the common period August 30, 2022–October 7, 2026, quarterly rebalancing, and an S&P 500 Total Return benchmark. Their recorded annualized returns were 10.03% and 10.05%; their 1-year Sharpe ratios were 1.41 and 1.69. Return, volatility, capture, and drawdown measurements use different windows and should not be conflated.

This page summarizes the supplied research conclusions and all five modern portfolio exports. It does not reproduce the full return histories, proxy substitutions, or all test assumptions, and does not independently rerun or audit the backtests. Historical comparisons are sensitive to dates, proxy choices, and implementation assumptions. No optimality claim is made.
