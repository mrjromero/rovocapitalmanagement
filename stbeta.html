What is this tool?
This tool bridges the gap between your portfolio's Leverage (Debt-to-Asset / DTA Ratio) and your M1 "Current balance and limits" screen: Borrow Capacity (Margin Utilization Rate).

Why does M1 have two different modes?
The platform calculates your borrow boundaries using two distinct risk frameworks:

• Withdrawal Basis (Cash Extraction Framework): Use this when taking physical cash out of the account or modeling automated Smart Transfer Rules. Because cash leaves the Invest overall account pie entirely, M1 enforces a strict, rigid credit ceiling.

• Reinvestment Basis (Collateralized Buying Power): Use this only when using margin to buy more stocks/funds inside M1. The platform is showing a synthetic/calculated ceiling rather than a raw, absolute regulatory limit because the newly bought shares stay inside the account as extra backup collateral.

4. Smart Transfer Guide & Guardrails

M1 Smart Transfers are strictly triggered by cash balance thresholds (fixed dollar boundaries) rather than percentage parameters. However, when an automation rule executes a sweep from your credit line, it is governed by specific structural guardrails:

  • Withdrawal Limit Dependency (Cash Extraction): Automated rules treat sweeps as capital extractions. Because the cash leaves the portfolio shell, the maximum transfer size is strictly capped by your Available for Withdrawal credit limit.
  • Reinvestment Rules Ignored: The expanded internal buying power ("Available to Invest") is completely bypassed by Smart Transfers since no balancing stock collateral is added duly to the account.
  • How to use for Smart Transfer calculations: Keep the toggle set to Withdrawal Basis. Input different target cash sweep amounts as simulated debt into Card 2 to see exactly what your dashboard Margin Utilization Rate and overall DTA will look like post-transfer, ensuring your automated rules never cross critical risk limits. You may also change the simulator to the desired DTA or Margin Utilization Rate to convert the values bi-directionally.
